📖 What You Must Know for Your Exam (Slide 23 Breakdown)

1. Why the Commutator is Cut in Half

An electromagnet has fixed North and South poles as long as current flows in one direction. As Wire A passes Stator North, it is repelled toward Stator South. Once Wire A reaches South ($90^\circ$), it wants to stay there! Splitting the ring allows the contacts to physically switch sides every $180^\circ$.

2. The Exact Moment of the Flip ($90^\circ$ & $270^\circ$)

When the coil is perfectly vertical, the insulating gap slides across the stationary brushes. For a fraction of a millisecond, current stops completely ($I = 0$). Momentum / inertia carries the heavy armature past this neutral zone.

3. What Happens Right After $90^\circ$ ($91^\circ$)

Segment A now connects to the Negative Brush, and Segment B connects to the Positive Brush! Current in the coil reverses direction. Wire A instantly flips from a North pole into a South pole! Now Stator South repels Wire A onwards, maintaining continuous clockwise rotation.

4. The Defective Solid Ring Result

Without the split, current never reverses. The motor will spin to $90^\circ$, rock back and forth, and freeze locked in place because opposite magnetic poles attract and stay pinned together.
